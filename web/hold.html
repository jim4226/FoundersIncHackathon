What you're looking at

  • Real scale. px/mm is derived from index-to-pinky knuckle span, not from a slider. 118 mm on screen is 118 mm on the desk.
  • Real pose. The part is seated on a basis built from wrist and knuckles, so it rolls with the hand instead of sliding on it.
  • Real occlusion. The camera's own pixels are re-drawn over the fingers, so the hand closes over the part.
  • Two cameras, two jobs. The lid camera watches your hand here; the desk camera watches the table. They run in separate processes, so both are live at once — as long as neither grabs the other's device.

Geometry is parametric, generated in millimetres from the same numbers as the STEP file. No mesh assets, no WebGL, no CDN for the render itself — only the hand tracker is fetched, and it degrades to a simulated hand when it can't be.
